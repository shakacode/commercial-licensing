# Open legal points

ShakaCode approved The ShakaStack License 1.0 and the coverage notice on
2026-11-11 without a counsel review. The points below remain open questions
for any later legal review. They no longer appear as markers in the license
text or the notice. Clause references are to the compiled files in
`releases/1.0/`.

| # | Clause | Open question |
|---|---|---|
| COUNSEL-1 | A2 "ShakaStack Pro" | The subscription is defined by its commercial name and by a channel ShakaCode designates in writing. Is that enough to bind the licensor to the scope in A6, and to let ShakaCode move the purchase address later without a new license version? Should a designation have to be published, not merely written? |
| COUNSEL-2 | A2 "Version line", A12 | The released texts promise that "patch and security releases of a version line remain under that line's terms" without defining version line. This text defines it as major.minor, prospectively, and does not reinterpret the old phrase. react-on-rails-rsc already binds its major.minor to the React runtime line; the other products have no written rule. Is it safe to ship this text in the next minor of each product (ShakaPerf 0.8.0, React on Rails Pro 17.3.0, Inertia Rails SSR Cache 0.2.0, react-on-rails-rsc at its next React runtime minor)? If counsel prefers the next major, the three products move there and no other text changes; react-on-rails-rsc then waits for a real new runtime line, because a license-only renumbering breaks its version policy. The coverage notice works either way. |
| COUNSEL-3 | A6 "Included Products" | The three named products stay included for releases under this text whenever a Subscription is active, including after renewal, and a later designation can add a product without changing delivered terms. Confirm this reads as intended, and that a promise in a license rather than in order terms is the right place for it. |
| COUNSEL-4 | A6 "Existing subscriptions" | Every React on Rails Pro subscription sold before this text is declared a ShakaStack Pro subscription. Those customers bought under EULA 2.3 or License 3.0 order terms. Can a later license text extend their entitlement without a countersignature? Should the Stripe product name and the invoice template change to match? |
| COUNSEL-5 | A16 | The schedule isolation rule stops the argument "React on Rails Pro CI is free, ShakaPerf is part of ShakaStack, so ShakaPerf CI is free". Is the wording strong enough against a claim that the products form one offering under one license name, so that the broadest free use applies across it? If not, the structure supports three separately named licenses with identical common text: only the title line changes. |
| COUNSEL-6 | ShakaPerf B2 | "A person's use of the Software on an organization's code or for an organization's work is use by that organization, not a personal project." Is this a clarification or a narrowing, compared with The ShakaPerf License 1.0, for a developer who runs ShakaPerf by hand on an employer's private repository? If a narrowing, the sentence can go, and A1 plus B2(e) carry the point. |
| COUNSEL-7 | A2 "Evaluation Period", A4(b) | One period of 45 consecutive days per organization per Product, from the first use that would require a Subscription without the evaluation permission, with time under earlier terms counted and independently free use excluded from the start. Is Product a clear enough unit to stop a restart per package? Does this survive a large group that first used a product years ago in one subsidiary, where the start date cannot be proved? |
| COUNSEL-8 | A5 | The released texts let the paid list override the evaluation, education, and public report permissions, which can swallow a genuine evaluation. This text removes that override: an express Free Use prevails over the examples of Covered Use, and each Free Use covers only what it states. Confirm the limits inside A4 and B3 close the laundering routes without the override. |
| COUNSEL-9 | A7 | The hosted offering clause now says "builds, runs, tests, or measures other organizations' applications" so that it covers a measurement tool. Is that broad enough, and does the separate agreement exception work for a consulting firm that runs ShakaPerf for clients under the client work rule in A5? |
| COUNSEL-10 | A8 | The true-up numbers (60 days outgrow grace, 60 days good faith discovery, 24 months back fees for bad faith) are unchanged from the released texts. This is their first outside review. |
| COUNSEL-11 | Title line and SPDX | The license is titled "The ShakaStack License, version 1.0, applied to <Product>". Each edition has its own identifier (`LicenseRef-ShakaStack-1.0-ShakaPerf`, `-ReactOnRailsPro`, `-InertiaRailsSsrCache`) because the complete texts differ in Part B. Is that the right form? |
| COUNSEL-12 | `notices/2026-11-shakastack-pro-coverage.md` | The notice extends ShakaStack Pro coverage to releases under the old texts: The Inertia Rails SSR Cache License 1.0 caps coverage at under 250 people and under US $10M, and EULA 2.3 licenses one legal entity. It is a unilateral grant of broader rights by the licensor. Confirm it needs no acceptance and no license version bump, that it works as the separate written agreement the Inertia text requires, and that publishing it here and linking it from the purchase page is sufficient notice. This one page can be read ahead of the rest. |
| COUNSEL-13 | A6 "Scope" | "No limit based on headcount, revenue, capital raised, or any other measure of size" is deliberately absolute, across the whole control group. Confirm it does not conflict with the order terms at pro.reactonrails.com or the Stripe product description. |
| COUNSEL-14 | A3 and B5 | The grant is split: A3 gives the core grant and B5 limits distribution per product. ShakaPerf may be redistributed under the same terms; the two runtime libraries may not be distributed standalone. Confirm the split reads as one grant and that the broader ShakaPerf right is intended to continue. |
| COUNSEL-15 | A4(c), A4(d), A13 | Three clauses now apply to products whose released text lacked them: the machine-learning carve-out in education and the "not for any organization's work" limit on personal projects reach the runtime products, and the feedback license reaches ShakaPerf. Are these acceptable as common terms for new releases, or should any stay product specific? |
| COUNSEL-16 | A6 "Order terms" | The license promises reasonable support with no guaranteed response time, while the purchase page and shakacode.com advertise Slack support and a 24-hour response. Should that language become an order term, or change? Do customers who bought on that promise keep it? |
| COUNSEL-17 | A14, A15 | First outside review of the disclaimer, the liability cap where the licensee paid nothing, group fees inside the cap, Hawaii law and venue, and assignment on a merger. |

Not reopened (decided by the CEO on 2026-11-04): common terms across the products,
one subscription for the whole organization, no 250 person or US $10M limit, a shared
versioned source, released versions keep their terms.
