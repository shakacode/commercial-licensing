# ShakaStack License changelog

## 1.0 (approved 2026-11-11)

The first common text. It replaces three product licenses for new releases only.
Every released version keeps the exact text it shipped with (see `archive/`).

### Common to every product

- The subscription is ShakaStack Pro. One subscription covers every Included
  Product for the whole organization, with no limit based on headcount, revenue,
  capital raised, or any other measure of size.
- Existing React on Rails Pro subscriptions and any other paid subscription to an
  Included Product count as ShakaStack Pro while active, at no additional charge.
- "Production use" as the common trigger is replaced by "Covered Use", which each
  Product Schedule defines. Section A16 stops the free uses of one product from
  applying to another.
- "Version line" is defined as major.minor.
- The Evaluation Period is one period of 45 consecutive days per organization per
  Product, starting at the first use that would require a Subscription without the
  evaluation permission. Earlier texts named no start and did not say consecutive.
  It does not restart with a version, person, installation, application, repository,
  package, or affiliate, and time used under earlier terms counts, which can reduce
  the evaluation left when adopting a new release.
- An express Free Use now prevails over the examples of Covered Use. The earlier
  override, which let the paid list defeat a genuine evaluation, is gone, and each
  Free Use states its own limits.
- The three named products stay included for releases under this text whenever a
  Subscription is active. A product added later joins by a published designation,
  which cannot remove a product or add a fee during a paid period.
- The coverage in Section A6 prevails over a narrower standard product or size limit
  in an order. Other order terms keep governing payment, renewal, and services. An
  earlier text let any conflicting order control.
- Cancelling a future renewal does not end a service period already paid for.
- No charge per repository or per run, in addition to per seat and per application.
- Curing a lapse excludes periods licensed or forgiven by the grace and good faith
  rules, and any Free Use.
- Personal projects are limited to an individual's own projects, not carried out
  for any organization's work.
- The exclusion of training or operating machine-learning models or agents from
  the education permission, already in the ShakaPerf text, now reaches the runtime
  products.
- Contractors count toward the Small Organization headcount.
- A license key, a registration, or a status label is not a Subscription.
- A feedback license applies to every product.
- The hosted offering clause covers building, running, testing, and measuring
  other organizations' applications. A contractor doing work for an identified
  client is not offering a prohibited service. Selling other organizations access
  to the Software's functionality needs a separate agreement, even when they are
  your customers.
- Client attribution covers testing, measuring, and other deliverables, in addition
  to building and running applications.
- The open-source permission reads the same for every product: developing, testing,
  operating, and supporting a qualifying public project, with the Software running
  as part of its public code or on it.

### ShakaPerf, compared with The ShakaPerf License 1.0

- The Software is now defined and lists the four npm packages.
- The definition of paid use keeps its shape: any use other than a Free Use. It now
  says in terms that interactive runs and every target environment are included.
- The tier reference to published pricing is gone. There are no tiers.
- An active Subscription now includes software updates and reasonable support
  through published channels. The earlier text promised neither.
- The license no longer says registration needs a verified email address.
  Registration stays optional. The ban on misrepresenting status now reaches the
  Software's configuration.
- The latest published shaka-bundle-size and shaka-accessibility declare MIT. Their
  next releases under this text add subscription, redistribution, and report terms.
  Published MIT releases stay MIT.

### React on Rails Pro, compared with The React on Rails Pro License 3.0

- Production now names the rendering, streaming, and background work that supports
  a live application, and an environment doing that work is production whatever its
  name. This can narrow a reading of the earlier free environments.
- Copies may be shared with contractors and service providers working for the
  organization generally, not only those working on its applications.
- `react-on-rails-rsc` is listed as a component of the Software.

### Inertia Rails SSR Cache, compared with The Inertia Rails SSR Cache License 1.0

- The limit of under 250 people and under US $10,000,000 in revenue is removed.
- Production now names warming and guarding a live application's cache, and an
  environment doing that work is production whatever its name. This can narrow a
  reading of the earlier free environments.
- Copies may be shared with contractors and service providers working for the
  organization generally, not only those working on its applications.
- The registration invitation returns, pointing at pro.reactonrails.com.
