# Free & Pro FAQ

This FAQ summarizes The ShakaStack License 1.0, which is proposed text pending counsel
review, and the ShakaStack Pro coverage notice. The complete license delivered with a
release controls that release.

## What does ShakaStack Pro include?

React on Rails Pro, ShakaPerf, and Inertia Rails SSR Cache for your whole organization:
every affiliate, application, environment, repository, pipeline, and developer. React on
Rails Pro includes its Node renderer and the react-on-rails-rsc package. There is no limit
on headcount or revenue. The price is US $1,800 per year per organization at
https://pro.reactonrails.com/.

## Is our organization free?

Answer three questions for the whole organization, counting all affiliates. Do fewer than
10 individuals perform paid work for it or on its products and services, whether directly or
through staffing, outsourcing, contracting, or services arrangements, counted as full-time
equivalents? Was its gross revenue for the twelve months ending with the last completed calendar quarter under
US $1M? Has it received under US $1M in outside capital, ever, as Section A2 defines it? If
all three answers are yes, its use is free. Government entities other than public
educational institutions cannot qualify this way.

Charities, educational institutions, and hospitals are free at any size. That status reaches
an affiliate only if the affiliate qualifies on its own. For client work, the client's status
decides. The limits on redistribution and hosted offerings still apply.

## Are development and CI free?

It depends on the product, because the products do different jobs. React on Rails Pro and
Inertia Rails SSR Cache run inside your application, so what needs a subscription is
production: serving real users, or background work that supports a live application,
whatever the environment is called. Environments used solely for development, test, CI,
staging, preview, or review before production are free for everyone, without a time limit.

ShakaPerf is a development tool. It never serves your users, so it has no production in that
sense. For ShakaPerf, the paid use is ongoing work for your organization: CI, pull-request
checks, coding-agent workflows, regression gates, private reports, and runs by hand on your
private code after the evaluation, against any environment. It stays free for organizations
under the free line, for a genuine evaluation, and for qualifying public open-source work.
Each product's free uses apply to that product only.

## What if we only run ShakaPerf by hand, or only against staging?

Neither the way ShakaPerf is started nor the environment it measures makes it free. Above the
free line, ongoing ShakaPerf work needs ShakaStack Pro after a genuine evaluation, unless
another free use or a grace period applies. A standalone qualifying public report is different
from publishing the output of ongoing private work.

## Can we evaluate with agents and private repositories?

Yes. Each organization gets one evaluation of up to 45 consecutive days per product. It can
include CI, agents, private repositories, and internal reports. It starts at the first use
that would otherwise need a subscription, and it does not restart with a new version,
package, person, or affiliate. Free development use of React on Rails Pro or Inertia Rails SSR
Cache does not start it.

## Can publishing a report make ShakaPerf free?

A standalone report made by hand is free if it stays public and discoverable for at least a
year and links to ShakaCode. It cannot be part of an automated workflow or of a workflow that
gates development or release. Publishing the output of ongoing private work does not make that
work free.

## Who pays for client work?

The organization whose business the work ultimately serves. It must qualify for free use or
hold a subscription, and contractors work under its coverage. An agency's own subscription
covers the agency's own work, not its clients.

## We already pay for React on Rails Pro. Do we need to do anything?

No. Your subscription counts as ShakaStack Pro while it stays active. It covers ShakaPerf and
Inertia Rails SSR Cache for your whole organization at no extra charge: on earlier releases
through the coverage notice, once that notice is in effect, and on new releases through the
license. Earlier releases keep their other terms.

## Does a sponsorship or a free key count?

An active paid sponsorship counts only if ShakaCode designated it as a software subscription to
one of the products, including under an earlier product name. Other sponsorships, donations, and supporter memberships are appreciated, and they
grant no rights. A free license key is not a subscription: it only changes the status label on
React on Rails Pro pages.

## Do large organizations need a separate agreement?

No. ShakaStack Pro has no size limit. A separate agreement is for what the standard
subscription does not cover: a hosted or embedded offering to other organizations, or
indemnity, support commitments, and procurement paperwork.

## Is a key or registration required?

No. No product needs a key to run, and registration is free and optional. There is no
activation, no usage telemetry, and no lockout. React on Rails Pro can use an optional key that
sets its attribution status to `Licensed`.

## What happens if a subscription ends?

For a release under The ShakaStack License 1.0, covered use stays licensed for 30 days while
you renew or wind down, and the software keeps running either way. A version's terms are
permanent, and they still require a subscription for covered use. Earlier releases keep their
own lapse and cure rules.

## What if we grow, or find a mistake?

Under The ShakaStack License 1.0 you have 60 days after outgrowing the free line. If you
discover in good faith that past use needed a subscription, buying one within 60 days of
discovery is full compliance, with no back payment. Section A8 has the details. Earlier releases
keep their own rules.

## Do the terms change on a version we already have?

No. Every release keeps the terms it shipped with. A patch or security release of a version
line stays under that line's terms. Earlier releases published under the MIT License remain
under it.

## Are the MIT projects part of this license?

No. React on Rails core, Inertia Rails, and the other open-source projects keep their own
licenses.
